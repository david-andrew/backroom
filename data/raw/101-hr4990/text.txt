A BILL
To require the Attorney General of the United States to promptly increase
activity with respect to the criminal referrals involving savings associations
which are pending before the Department of Justice, to require quarterly
appearances by the Attorney General before committees of the Congress to
report on progress being made in investigating and prosecuting cases involving
fraud and embezzlement in savings associations, and for other purposes.
 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United
 States of America in Congress assembled,
SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.
 This Act may be cited as the `Savings Association Law Enforcement Improvement
 Act of 1990'.
SEC. 2. FINDINGS.
 The Congress hereby finds the following:
 (1) The Financial Institutions Reform, Recovery, and Enforcement Act of 1989
 authorized an appropriation to the Attorney General of $75,000,000 for each
 of fiscal years 1990, 1991, and 1992 to fund civil and criminal enforcement
 activity under such Act and other Federal banking and criminal statutes.
 (2) Despite the enormous backlog of fraud and embezzlement referrals and
 cases, the Attorney General sought an appropriation of only $50,000,000
 of the amount authorized by such Act.
 (3) The Attorney General has approved only 47 percent of the number
 of additional agents requested by the Federal Bureau of Investigation
 for investigating cases of savings and loan fraud, and 51 percent of
 the assistant United States attorneys requested for investigating and
 prosecuting such cases.
 (4) Despite the enormity of the task, the Attorney General has proposed
 no increase in the number of criminal division attorneys or assistant
 United States attorneys for fraud and embezzlement matters in fiscal
 year 1991 and only minimal increases in the number of Federal Bureau of
 Investigation personnel.
 (5) A number of United States attorney's offices have received no new
 personnel since the enactment of the Financial Institutions Reform, Recovery,
 and Enforcement Act of 1989 to assist in the prosecution of numerous cases
 involving losses of $100,000 or more.
 (6) As of February 1990, the Federal Bureau of Investigation had received
 over 21,000 referrals and complaints relating to savings and loan misconduct
 on which the Bureau had taken no action.
 (7) Of the referrals referred to in paragraph (6), 234 involve potential
 losses of $1,000,000 or more.
SEC. 3. SAVINGS ASSOCIATION LAW ENFORCEMENT IMPROVEMENT.
 (a) INCREASED ACTION IN PENDING CASE REFERRALS- The Attorney General shall
 take such action as may be necessary to ensure that appropriate action is
 initiated with respect to investigating or prosecuting--
 (1) not less than 1/2 of the referrals and complaints relating to criminal
 misconduct involving savings associations (as defined in section 3(b)(1) of
 the Federal Deposit Insurance Act) which are pending before, or are otherwise
 within the jurisdiction of, any agency or office of the Department of
 Justice on the date of the enactment of this Act before January 1, 1991; and
 (2) the remainder of the referrals and complaints described in paragraph
 (1) before January 1, 1992.
 (b) INITIATIVES REQUIRED TO INCREASE RECOVERY OF LOSSES- The Attorney
 General shall take such action as may be appropriate to recover, or obtain
 restitution for, amounts lost as a result of fraud or embezzlement by any
 person from savings associations (as defined in section 3(b)(1) of the
 Federal Deposit Insurance Act).
SEC. 4. APPEARANCES BEFORE CONGRESS.
 The Attorney General shall appear before the Committee on Banking, Finance
 and Urban Affairs of the House of Representatives and the Committee on
 Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs of the Senate at least once during
 each calendar quarter beginning after the date of the enactment of this
 Act to report on--
 (1) the number and status of investigations being conducted by grand juries
 and the Federal Bureau of Investigation which involve savings associations
 (as defined in section 3(b)(1) of the Federal Deposit Insurance Act);
 (2) the number and disposition of prosecutions and civil actions commenced
 by the Attorney General which involve savings associations; and
 (3) the progress made in recovering, or obtaining restitution for,
 amounts lost as a result of fraud or embezzlement by any person from
 savings associations.
