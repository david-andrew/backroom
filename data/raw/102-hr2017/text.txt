A BILL
To provide for the disclosure of certain information relating to public
hazards.
 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United
 States of America in Congress assembled,
SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.
 This Act may be cited as the `Federal Sunshine in Litigation Act of 1990'.
SEC. 2. LIMITATION ON ISSUANCE OF PROTECTIVE ORDERS.
 No order shall issue under that portion of the Federal Rules of Civil
 Procedure relating to protective orders respecting discovery that has the
 purpose or effect of concealing information about a public hazard.
SEC. 3. AGREEMENTS CONCEALING PUBLIC HAZARDS.
 Any portion of an agreement in settlement of a case in Federal court that
 has the purpose or effect of concealing information about a public hazard
 is void and may not be enforced.
SEC. 4. BROAD STANDING TO OBTAIN DISCLOSURE.
 (a) IN GENERAL- Any person who is substantially affected by an order or
 agreement prohibited by this Act may, by motion or in a civil action,
 obtain the disclosure of that portion of the information covered by that
 order or agreement that is about a public hazard.
 (b) MEDIA SUBSTANTIALLY AFFECTED- For the purposes of this section, the
 news media are substantially affected by any order or agreement prohibited
 by this Act.
SEC. 5. IN CAMERA PROCEDURE.
 In any proceeding to enforce the prohibitions of this Act, the court shall
 examine the disputed information in camera.
SEC. 6. USE OF COPYRIGHT TO EVADE THIS ACT.
 The copywriting of information under title 17, United States Code, does
 not prevent the disclosure or use of that information to the extent the
 copywrite would have the purpose or effect of concealing information about
 a public hazard.
SEC. 7. DEFINITION OF PUBLIC HAZARD.
 As used in this Act, the term `public hazard' means any condition,
 circumstance, person, or thing whatsoever that has caused damage and is
 likely to do so again.
