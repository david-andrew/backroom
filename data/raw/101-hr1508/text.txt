A BILL
To terminate the tolerance for daminozide under the Federal Food, Drug,
and Cosmetic Act.
 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United
 States of America in Congress assembled,
SECTION 1. FINDINGS.
 The Congress finds that--
 (1) children are more vulnerable to adverse health effects from pesticide
 residues in food because they consume proportionately more fruits and
 vegetables than adults and their bodies are still in developmental stages,
 (2) the Environmental Protection Agency has found daminozide's metabolite,
 UDMH, to be a potent human carcinogen,
 (3) the risk to children from daminozide and its metabolite, UDMH, even
 during the relatively short time it will take to remove the pesticide from
 the market, is unacceptably high,
 (4) the Environmental Protection Agency, under its authority under the
 Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, can prohibit the use of daminozide
 on food and it has failed to do so, and
 (5) in order to protect the health of children of the United States,
 the use of the pesticide daminozide on food must be immediately prohibited.
SEC. 2. TERMINATION OF TOLERANCE.
 Not later than the expiration of 90 days after the date of the enactment
 of this Act, any tolerance for daminozide in effect under section 408 of
 the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act shall no longer be in effect.
