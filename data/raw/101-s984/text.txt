A BILL
To amend the Motor Vehicle Information and Cost Savings Act.
 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United
 States of America in Congress assembled,
SHORT TITLE; FINDINGS
 SECTION 1. (a) This Act may be cited as the `Automobile Fuel Economy Act
 of 1989'.
 (b) The Congress finds that--
 (1) on January 3, 1989, President Reagan transmitted to Congress an official
 finding under section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act that `. . . petroleum
 imports threaten to impair the national security', and since then President
 Bush has expressed his concern that rising oil imports are a threat to
 the national security;
 (2) the release of carbon dioxide by passenger vehicles is increasingly
 suspected as significantly contributing to global changes in the earth's
 atmosphere;
 (3) while government energy forecasters had assumed that new car fuel
 efficiencies would continue to rise through the turn of the century there
 is concern that these expected improvements in fuel efficiency will not
 be forthcoming;
 (4) analyses by the Department of Energy and the Office of Technology
 Assessment indicate that known, cost-effective technologies already in
 use in automobiles today could, if implemented to a greater extent in the
 new car fleet, provide substantial improvement in new car fuel economy
 without requiring shifts in the size mix, performance, or luxury equipment
 of new cars;
 (5) interim CAFE standards for the period through 1996 could be satisfied
 by the use of these known technologies using existing vehicle production
 facilities; and
 (6) as we learn more about the threat of global warming, and the energy
 savings potential of future technologies, and as our energy security
 situation unfolds, these interim CAFE standards may require adjustment.

ANTI-BACKSLIDING
 SEC. 3. (a) Section 507(a) of the Motor Vehicle Information and Cost
 Savings Act (15 U.S.C. 2007(a)) is amended--
 (1) by deleting `or' at the end of paragraph (2);
 (2) by deleting the period at the end of paragraph (3) and inserting in
 lieu thereof a comma and `or'; and
 (3) by adding at the end thereof the following new paragraph:
 `(4) beginning with model year 1991 and each model year thereafter, the
 failure of any manufacturer to achieve with respect to each such model year
 an average fuel economy for such model year, for the category described in
 section 503(b)(1)(B), at least equal to what is achieved for such category
 in at least one of the 2 model years preceding such model year.'
 (b) Section 508(a) (1) and (2) of the Motor Vehicle Information and Cost
 Savings Act (15 U.S.C. 2008(a) (1) and (2)) are amended by inserting
 immediately after `or (2)' each place it appears therein `or (4)'.
 (c) Section 508(b)(1) of the Motor Vehicle Information and Cost Savings Act
 (15 U.S.C. 2008(b)(1)) is amended by adding at the end thereof the following:
 `(C) Any manufacturer whom the Secretary determines under subsection (a)
 to have violated section 507(a)(4) shall be liable to the United States for
 a civil penalty equal to (i) $10 for each tenth of a mile per gallon by
 which the average fuel economy of the passenger automobiles manufactured
 by such manufacturer during the model year in which the violation occurs
 falls below the lowest average fuel economy that manufacturer achieved
 in the two previous model years, multiplied by (ii), the total number of
 passenger automobiles which are manufactured by such manufacturer during
 the model year in which the violation occurs.'
PRESCRIPTION OF ADDITIONAL AMOUNTS
 SEC. 4. Section 508 of the Motor Vehicle Information and Cost Savings Act
 (15 U.S.C. 2008) is amended--
 (1) in subsection (b)(1) by deleting `$5' each place it appears therein
 and inserting in lieu thereof `$10';
 (2) in subsection (d)(2) by deleting `$5.00' and inserting in lieu thereof
 `$10.00', and by deleting `$10.00' and inserting in lieu thereof `$20.00';
 and
 (3) by adding at the end thereof the following new subsection:
 `(f) Beginning with model year 1990, each model year thereafter, if
 the Secretary finds that any manufacturer has exhibited a pattern of
 noncompliance with any provision of this Act by violating, with respect
 to model year 1990 or any model year thereafter, the same provision which
 such manufacturer has previously violated in each of three consecutive
 model years, including model years prior to 1990, then the Secretary shall
 double the otherwise applicable civil penalty upon such manufacturer for
 each violation occurring with respect to model year 1990 and each model
 year thereafter.'.
REPORT
 SEC. 5. No later than 1 year after the date of the enactment of this Act,
 the Secretary of Transportation and the Secretary of Energy shall report to
 the Congress on the availability of existing fuel-saving technologies and
 the potential for developing technologies by the year 2000 for light-duty
 passenger vehicles. No later than 6 months after the date of the enactment
 of this Act, the Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency shall
 report to the Congress on the accuracy and reliability of the Environmental
 Protection Agency's fuel economy testing procedures.
