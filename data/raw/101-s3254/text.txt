A BILL
To protect the free exercise of religion.
 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United
 States of America in Congress assembled,
SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.
 This Act may be cited as the `Religious Freedom Restoration Act of 1990'.
SEC. 2. FREE EXERCISE OF RELIGION.
 (a) IN GENERAL- Except as provided in subsection (b), the government of
 the United States, a State, or a subdivision of a State may not restrict
 any person's free exercise of religion.
 (b) LAWS OF GENERAL APPLICABILITY- A government may restrict a person's
 free exercise of religion only if--
 (1) the restriction--
 (A) is an otherwise valid statute, ordinance, or other form of rule of
 general applicability or action taken to enforce such a rule; and
 (B) does not intentionally discriminate against religion or among religions;
 and
 (2) the government demonstrates that application of the restriction to
 the person--
 (A) is essential to further a compelling governmental interest; and
 (B) is the least restrictive means of furthering that compelling governmental
 interest.
 (c) CIVIL ACTION- A person aggrieved by a violation of this section may
 obtain appropriate relief (including relief against a government) in a
 civil action.
SEC. 3. ATTORNEY'S FEES.
 (a) JUDICIAL PROCEEDINGS- Section 722 of the Revised Statutes (42
 U.S.C. 1988) is amended by inserting `the Religious Freedom Restoration
 Act of 1990,' before `or title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964'.
 (b) ADMINISTRATIVE PROCEEDINGS- Section 504(b)(1)(C) of title 5, United
 States Code, is amended--
 (1) by striking `and' at the end of clause (ii):
 (2) by striking the semicolon at the end of clause (iii) and inserting `;
 and'; and
 (3) by inserting `(iv) the Religious Freedom Restoration Act of 1990;'
 after clause (iii).
SEC. 4. DEFINITIONS.
 As used in this Act--
 (1) the term `government' includes a branch, department, agency,
 instrumentality, and official (or other person acting under color of law)
 of the United States, a State, or a subdivision of a State;
 (2) the term `State' means a State, the District of Columbia, the
 Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, and each territory and possession of the
 United States;
 (3) the term `demonstrates' means meets the burdens of going forward with
 the evidence and of persuasion; and
 (4) the term `person' includes natural persons, religious organizations,
 associations, and corporations.
SEC. 5. RULE OF CONSTRUCTION.
 (a) IN GENERAL- This Act applies to--
 (1) every Federal, State, and local statute, ordinance, regulation,
 administrative order, decision, practice, and other action enacted, adopted,
 or implemented on or before the date of enactment of this Act; and
 (2) every State and local statute, ordinance, regulation, administrative
 order, decision, practice, and other action enacted, adopted, or implemented
 after the date of enactment of this Act.
 (b) FUTURE FEDERAL LEGISLATION- This Act shall apply to a Federal statute
 enacted after the date of enactment of this Act unless the statute, by
 specific reference to this Act, expressly exempts the statute or part of
 it from the application of this Act.
 (c) FUTURE FEDERAL ADMINISTRATIVE ACTION- This Act shall apply to a
 Federal regulation, administrative order, decision, practice, or other
 action adopted or implemented after the date of enactment of this Act
 unless it is adopted or implemented under authority of a statute or part
 of a statute that makes an exemption in accordance with subsection (b).
SEC. 6. ESTABLISHMENT CLAUSE UNAFFECTED.
 Nothing in this Act limits or creates rights under the clause of the first
 article of amendment to the Constitution that prohibits laws respecting
 an establishment of religion.
