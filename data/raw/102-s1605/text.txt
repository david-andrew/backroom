SECTION 1. ENVIRONMENTAL CRIMES.
 Title 18 of the United States Code is amended by inserting after chapter
 33 the following new chapter:
`CHAPTER 34--ENVIRONMENTAL CRIMES
`Sec.
`731. Knowingly or recklessly endangering life or causing environmental
catastrophe.
`732. Endangering life or causing environmental catastrophe by a course of
illegal conduct.
`733. Negligently endangering life or causing environmental catastrophe.
`734. Environmental compliance audit.
`735. Definitions.
`Sec. 731. Knowingly or recklessly endangering life or causing environmental
catastrophe
 `(a) OFFENSE- Whoever commits an environmental offense and thereby knowingly
 or recklessly creates an increased risk of--
 `(1) imminent death of a human being;
 `(2) serious bodily injury to a human being; or
 `(3) environmental catastrophe,
shall, in addition to being punished for the underlying violation of a
statute listed in section 735(2), be punished as set forth in subsection (b).
 `(b) PUNISHMENT- (1) An offense under subsection (a)(1) shall be punished--
 `(A) for a first offense, by imprisonment for not more than 15 years, a fine
 of not less than $25,000 or more than $250,000 in the case of an individual
 and not more than $1,000,000 in the case of an organization, or both;
 `(B) for a second or subsequent offense, by imprisonment for not more than
 30 years, a fine of not less than $50,000 and not more than $500,000 in
 the case of an individual and not more than $2,000,000 in the case of an
 organization, or both; and
 `(C) for an offense under that subsection after a conviction for an offense
 under subsection (a) (2) or (3), by imprisonment for not more than 20 years,
 a fine of not less than $37,500 or more than $375,000 in the case of an
 individual and not more than $1,500,000 in the case of an organization,
 or both.
 `(2) An offense under subsection (a) (2) or (3) shall be punished--
 `(A) for a first offense, by imprisonment for not more than 10 years, a fine
 of not less than $25,000 or more than $250,000 in the case of an individual
 and not more than $750,000 in the case of an organization, or both; and
 `(B) for a second or subsequent offense, by imprisonment for not more
 than 20 years, a fine of not less than $50,000 or more than $500,000 in
 the case of an individual and not more than $1,500,000 in the case of an
 organization, or both.
 `(c) COURSE OF ILLEGAL CONDUCT- (1) Subject to paragraph (1), for the
 purposes of this section a course of illegal conduct consists of 2 or more
 environmental offenses, each of which contributes to the increased risk
 described in subsection (a).
 `(2) A single discrete occurrence that would constitute more than 1
 environmental offense under the statutes listed in section 735(2) shall
 be considered to be a single environmental offense for purposes of this
 subsection.
`Sec. 732. Endangering life or causing environmental catastrophe by a course
of illegal conduct
 `(a) OFFENSE- Whoever knowingly engages in a course of illegal conduct
 and thereby creates an increased risk of--
 `(1) imminent death of a human being;
 `(2) serious bodily injury to a human being; or
 `(3) environmental catastrophe,
shall, in addition to being punished for the underlying violation of a
statute listed in section 735(2), be punished as set forth in subsection (b).
 `(b) PUNISHMENT- (1) An offense under subsection (a)(1) shall be punished--
 `(A) for a first offense, by imprisonment for not more than 15 years, a fine
 of not less than $25,000 or more than $250,000 in the case of an individual
 and not more than $1,000,000 in the case of an organization, or both;
 `(B) for a second or subsequent offense, by imprisonment for not more than
 30 years, a fine of up to not less than $50,000 or more than $500,000 in
 the case of an individual and not more than $2,000,000 in the case of an
 organization, or both; and
 `(C) for an offense after a conviction for an offense under subsection (a)
 (2) or (3), by imprisonment for not more than 20 years, a fine of not less
 than $37,500 or more than $375,000 in the case of an individual and not
 more than $1,500,000 in the case of an organization, or both.
 `(2) An offense under subsection (a) (2) or (3) of this section shall
 be punished--
 `(A) for a first offense, by imprisonment for not more than 10 years, a fine
 of not less than $25,000 or more than $250,000 in the case of an individual
 and not more than $750,000 in the case of an organization, or both; and
 `(B) after a conviction for another offense under this section, by
 imprisonment for not more than 20 years, a fine of up to not less than
 $50,000 or more than $500,000 in the case of an individual and not more
 than $1,500,000 in the case of an organization, or both.
`Sec. 733. Negligently endangering life or causing environmental catastrophe
 `Whoever commits an environmental offense and thereby negligently causes
 a risk of--
 `(1) imminent death of a human being;
 `(2) serious bodily injury to a human being; or
 `(3) environmental catastrophe;
shall be imprisoned not more than 1 year or fined not less than $12,500 or
more than $125,000 in the case of an individual and not more than $500,000
in the case of an organization, or both.
`Sec. 734. Environmental compliance audit
 `(a) IN GENERAL- A court of the United States--
 `(1) shall, when sentencing an organization for an environmental offense
 that is a felony; and
 `(2) may, when sentencing an organization for a misdemeanor environmental
 offense,
require that the organization pay for an environmental compliance audit.
 `(b) APPOINTMENT OF INDEPENDENT EXPERT- The court shall appoint an
 independent expert--
 `(1) with no prior involvement in the management of the organization
 sentenced to conduct an environmental compliance audit under this section;
 and
 `(2) who has demonstrated abilities to properly conduct such audits.
 `(c) CONTENTS OF COMPLIANCE AUDIT- (1) An environmental compliance audit
 shall--
 `(A) identify all causes of and factors relating to the offense; and
 `(B) recommend specific measures that should be taken to prevent a recurrence
 of those causes and factors and avoid potential environmental offenses.
 `(2) An environmental compliance audit shall not recommend measures under
 paragraph (1)(B) that would require the violation of an environmental
 statute, regulation, or permit.
 `(d) COURT-ORDERED IMPLEMENTATION OF COMPLIANCE AUDIT- The court shall
 order the defendant to implement the appropriate recommendations of the
 environmental compliance audit.
 `(e) ADDITIONAL STANDING TO RAISE FAILURE TO IMPLEMENT COMPLIANCE AUDIT-
 (1) The prosecutor, auditor, any governmental agency, or any private
 individual may present evidence to the court that a defendant has failed
 to comply with the court order under subsection (d).
 `(2) When evidence of failure to comply with the court order under subsection
 (d) is presented pursuant to paragraph (1), the court shall consider all
 relevant evidence and, if the court determines that the defendant has not
 fully complied with the court order, order appropriate sanctions.
`Sec. 735. Definitions
 `For the purposes of this chapter--
 `(1) the term `environmental catastrophe' means--
 `(A) death or injury to 20 percent or more of the known population of any
 species of fish, wildlife, or plant within a defined ecosystem; or
 `(B) destruction of habitat of any species of fish, wildlife, plant, or
 other living natural resource that prohibits the ability of the habitat
 to support viable breeding of the affected species;
 `(2) the term `environmental offense' means a criminal violation of--
 `(A) the Clean Air Act (42 U.S.C. 7401 et seq.);
 `(B) the Federal Water Pollution Control Act (33 U.S.C. 1251 et
 seq.) (commonly known as the Clean Water Act);
 `(C) the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability
 Act of 1980 (42 U.S.C. 9601 et seq.);
 `(D) the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.);
 `(E) the Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act (7 U.S.C. 136
 et seq.);
 `(F) the Solid Waste Disposal Act (42 U.S.C. 6901 et seq.);
 `(G) title XIV of the Public Health Service Act (42 U.S.C. 300f et
 seq.) (commonly known as the Safe Drinking Water Act);
 `(H) the Toxic Substances Control Act (15 U.S.C. 2601 et seq.);
 `(I) the Lacey Act; and
 `(J) the Emergency Planning and Community Right-To-Know Act of 1986;
 `(4) except as they may be otherwise defined in the statutes listed in
 paragraph (2), the terms `knowingly' and `recklessly' mean--
 `(A) with awareness of a risk that a circumstance exists or a result
 will occur;
 `(B) the risk is of such a nature and degree and the circumstances are
 such that to disregard the risk constitutes a gross deviation from the
 standard of care that a reasonable person would exercise; and
 `(C) the person disregards the risk;
 `(5) except as it may be otherwise defined in the statutes listed in
 paragraph (2), the term `negligently' means--
 `(A) with unawareness of a risk that a circumstance exists or a result
 will occur; and
 `(B) the risk is of such a nature and degree and the circumstances are such
 that to be unaware of the risk constitutes a deviation from the standard
 of care that a reasonable person would exercise; and
 `(6) the term `species' includes any subspecies and any distinct population
 segment that interbreeds when mature.'.
