A BILL
To amend the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 to provide certain tax incentives
for domestic oil and gas, to establish a domestic petroleum production program,
to require the Strategic Petroleum Reserve to be filled with stripper well
oil, and to eliminate certain restrictions on the sale of natural gas.
 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United
 States of America in Congress assembled,
SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.
 This Act may be cited as the `Secure Energy Supply Act of 1989'.

SEC. 103. DEDUCTION TO CRUDE OIL PRODUCERS FOR CERTAIN LOSSES ON DOMESTIC
CRUDE OIL.
 (a) IN GENERAL- Part VI of subchapter B of chapter 1 of the Internal
 Revenue Code of 1986 (relating to itemized deductions for individuals
 and corporations) is amended by adding at the end thereof the following
 new section:
`SEC. 197. DEDUCTION TO CRUDE OIL PRODUCERS FOR CERTAIN LOSSES ON DOMESTIC
CRUDE OIL.
 `(a) ALLOWANCE OF DEDUCTION- There shall be allowed as a deduction for
 the taxable year to any producer of crude oil an amount equal to the
 applicable percentage of the qualified loss on each barrel of domestic
 crude oil produced by such producer which is removed from the premises
 during the taxable year.
 `(b) APPLICABLE PERCENTAGE; QUALIFIED LOSS- For purposes of subsection (a)--
 `(1) APPLICABLE PERCENTAGE- The term `applicable percentage' means--
 `(A) 100 percent in the case of oil which would (but for the repeal of
 chapter 45) be independent producer oil, and
 `(B) 80 percent for all other domestic crude oil.
 `(2) QUALIFIED LOSS- The term `qualified loss' means, with respect to any
 barrel of crude oil, the excess of--
 `(1) the sum of--
 `(A) the adjusted base price of such barrel, and
 `(B) the amount of the severance tax adjustment with respect to such
 barrel, over
 `(2) the removal price of such barrel.
 `(c) AGGREGATE DEDUCTION NOT TO EXCEED AGGREGATE WINDFALL PROFIT TAX PAID-
 The amount allowed as a deduction under subsection (a) to a producer for
 the taxable year shall not exceed the excess of--
 `(1) the aggregate amount of tax imposed by section 4986 (relating to
 windfall profit tax on domestic crude oil) paid by such producer as of
 the close of the preceding taxable year, over
 `(2) the aggregate deductions allowed under this section to such producer
 for all preceding taxable years.
 `(d) OTHER DEFINITIONS- For purposes of this section, terms used in this
 section which are also used in chapter 45 (as in effect before its repeal)
 shall have the respective meanings given such terms under such chapter
 (as so in effect).'
 (b) CLERICAL AMENDMENT- The table of sections for such part VI is amended
 by adding at the end thereof the following new item:
`Sec. 197. Deduction to crude oil producers for certain losses on domestic
crude oil.'
 (c) EFFECTIVE DATE- The amendments made by this section shall apply to
 oil removed after December 31, 1988, in taxable years ending after such date.
SEC. 102. MODIFICATION TO NET OPERATING LOSS RULES WITH RESPECT TO LOSSES
ON DOMESTIC CRUDE OIL.
 (a) 6-YEAR CARRYBACK- Paragraph (1) of section 172(b) of the Internal
 Revenue Code of 1986 (relating to net operating loss deduction) is amended
 by adding at the end thereof the following new subparagraph:
 `(M) CERTAIN DOMESTIC CRUDE OIL LOSSES- In the case of any taxpayer which
 has a qualified domestic crude oil loss (as defined in subsection (l))
 for any taxable year ending after December 31, 1988, such loss shall be
 a net operating loss carryback to each of the 6 taxable years preceding
 the taxable year of such loss.'
 (b) QUALIFIED DOMESTIC CRUDE OIL LOSS DEFINED- Section 172 of such Code is
 amended by redesignating subsection (m) as subsection (n) and by inserting
 after subsection (l) the following new subsection:
 `(m) DEFINITIONS AND SPECIAL RULES RELATING TO QUALIFIED DOMESTIC CRUDE
 OIL LOSSES- For purposes of this section--
 `(1) IN GENERAL- The term `qualified domestic crude oil loss' means,
 for any taxable year, the lesser of--
 `(A) the net operating loss for such taxable year, reduced by any portion
 thereof attributable to--
 `(i) a foreign expropriation loss,
 `(ii) a product liability loss, or
 `(iii) a deferred statutory or tort liability loss, or
 `(B) the sum of the amounts allowable as a deduction under section 197
 which is taken into account in computing the net operating loss for such
 taxable year.
 `(2) COORDINATION WITH SUBSECTION (b)(2)- In applying paragraph (2)
 of subsection (b), a qualified domestic crude oil loss shall be treated
 in a manner similar to the manner in which a foreign expropriation loss
 is treated.'
 (c) EFFECTIVE DATE- The amendments made by this section shall apply to
 taxable years ending after December 31, 1988.
TITLE II--DOMESTIC PETROLEUM PRODUCTION PROGRAM
SEC. 201. SHORT TITLE.
 This title may be cited as the `Domestic Petroleum Production Incentive
 Act of 1989'.
SEC. 202. DOMESTIC PETROLEUM PRODUCTION PROGRAM.
 (a) IN GENERAL- (1) Not later than 120 days after the date of the enactment
 of this title, the President shall promulgate a regulation providing for
 a domestic petroleum production program in accordance with this section.
 (2) Such regulation shall take effect not later than 120 days after the
 date on which it is promulgated.
 (b) CONTENTS- Such regulation shall--
 (1) require domestic refiners of petroleum products that are purchasing, on
 the date of the enactment of this title, crude oil from domestic producers
 not involved in the refining or marketing of petroleum products to continue
 to meet their crude oil requirements with such purchases to the extent
 crude oil is available from such producers;
 (2) establish a minimum wellhead price of $18 per barrel for the crude
 oil purchased pursuant to paragraph (1), adjusted for the location and
 quality of the crude oil involved in a particular transaction;
 (3) require each domestic refiner of petroleum products to pay, with respect
 to each barrel of crude oil purchased for less than $18, to the Secretary
 of the Treasury, for deposit into the Fund established by subsection (c),
 an amount equal to--
 (A) $18, minus
 (B) the average per barrel price paid for crude oil by such refiner
 calculated on the basis of the refiner's weighted average cost of crude
 oil purchased from all sources during the time period concerned; and
 (4) require importers of any imported refined petroleum product to pay
 to the Secretary of the Treasury, for deposit into the Fund described in
 subsection (c), an amount equal to--
 (A) the rack price for such refined petroleum product at the domestic
 refinery located nearest to the place of entry into the United States of
 such refined product, minus
 (B) the price paid for such imported product by the importer concerned.
 (c) FUND- (1) There is hereby established in the Treasury of the United
 States the Refiners' Blending Surcharge Fund (hereafter referred to as the
 `Fund').
 (2) There shall be deposited into the Fund the amounts paid to the Secretary
 of the Treasury pursuant to the regulation promulgated under subsection
 (a), as described in paragraphs (3) and (4) of subsection (b).
 (3)(A) Fifty percent of the amount in the Fund shall be considered to be
 part of the general revenues of the Treasury and available for appropriation
 by law.
 (B) Fifty percent of such amount shall be used to reduce the deficit in
 the budget of the United States, as specified by the President.
SEC. 203. ADMINISTRATION AND ENFORCEMENT.
 (a) ADMINISTRATION- The President may delegate all or any portion of the
 authority granted to him under this title to such officers, departments,
 or agencies of the United States, or to any State (or officer thereof),
 as the President deems appropriate.
 (b) ENFORCEMENT- (1) Whoever violates any provision of the regulation
 promulgated under section 202(a) or any order made thereunder shall be
 subject to a civil penalty of not more than $5,000 for each violation.
 (2) Whoever willfully violates any provision of such regulation or any
 such order shall be imprisoned not more than 1 year, or shall be fined
 not more than $10,000 for each violation, or both.
 (3) Any individual director, officer, or agent of a corporation who
 knowingly and willfully authorizes, orders, or performs any of the acts
 or practices constituting in whole or in part a violation of paragraph
 (2), shall be subject to penalties under this subsection without regard
 to any penalties to which that corporation may be subject under paragraph
 (2), except that no such individual director, officer, or agent shall be
 subject to imprisonment under such paragraph unless he also has knowledge,
 or reasonably should have known, of notice of noncompliance received by
 the corporation from the President.
TITLE III--FILLING STRATEGIC PETROLEUM RESERVE WITH STRIPPER WELL OIL
SEC. 301. STRATEGIC PETROLEUM RESERVE.
 (a) RESERVE TO BE FILLED WITH CRUDE OIL FROM STRIPPER WELLS OR OIL EXCHANGED
 FOR STRIPPER WELL OIL- Section 160 of the Energy Policy and Conservation
 Act (42 U.S.C. 6240) is amended by adding at the end the following new
 subsection:
 `(f) The Secretary shall provide that, after the date of the enactment of
 this subsection, the Strategic Petroleum Reserve will be filled only with--
 `(1) crude oil produced in the United States from stripper well property
 within the meaning of the June 1979 energy regulations as defined in section
 4996(b)(8) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1954 (as in effect before its
 repeal); or
 `(2) crude oil exchanged for the crude oil described in paragraph (1),
if the crude oil described in paragraph (1) can be obtained at, or for less
than, the price for which it can be obtained from other sources.'.
 (b) REAFFIRMATION OF INTENT TO FILL TO 750,000,000 BARRELS- The Congress
 reaffirms its intent that the Strategic Petroleum Reserve be filled to a
 level of at least 750,000,000 barrels.
 (c) AUTHORIZATION OF APPROPRIATIONS- In addition to other amounts authorized
 to be appropriated for the Strategic Petroleum Reserve, there are authorized
 to be appropriated for the acquisition, transportation, and injection of
 petroleum products into the Reserve amounts appropriated for any purpose
 before the date of the enactment of this Act which remain available for
 obligation.
TITLE IV--REMOVAL OF WELLHEAD PRICE CONTROLS AND REPEAL OF NATURAL GAS ACT
JURISDICTION OVER CERTAIN FIRST SALES OF NATURAL GAS
SEC. 401. REMOVAL OF WELLHEAD PRICE CONTROLS.
 Section 121 of the Natural Gas Policy Act of 1978 (15 U.S.C. 3331) is
 amended by striking out `Subject to the reimposition of price controls
 as provided in section 122, the' and inserting in lieu thereof `The',
 and by adding at the end thereof a new subsection as follows:
 `(f) SPECIAL RULE- The provisions of subtitle A shall not apply to--
 `(1) gas subject to any contract for the first sale of natural gas that
 was executed after the date of enactment of this subsection,
 `(2) gas subject to any contract for the first sale of natural gas that
 was renegotiated after the date of enactment of this subsection if such
 renegotiated contract expressly provides that the provisions of subtitle
 A shall not apply, or
 `(3) gas subject to any contract for the first sale of natural gas that
 expired, lapsed, or terminated pursuant to its own terms.'.
SEC. 402. REPEAL OF NATURAL GAS ACT JURISDICTION OVER CERTAIN SALES OF
COMMITTED OR DEDICATED NATURAL GAS.
 Section 601(a)(1)(B) of the Natural Gas Policy Act of 1978 (15
 U.S.C. 3431(a)(1)(B)) is amended by striking out `or' at the end of clause
 (ii), by striking out the period at the end of clause (iii) and inserting
 in lieu thereof `; or', and by adding at the end thereof a new clause
 as follows:
 `(iv) natural gas exempted from the operation of subtitle A of title I
 pursuant to section 121(f).'.
SEC. 403. REPEAL OF PROVISIONS ALLOWING REIMPOSITION OF PRICE CONTROLS AND
REPORT TO THE CONGRESS.
 (a) Sections 122 and 507 of the Natural Gas Policy Act of 1978 (15
 U.S.C. 3332 and 3417) are repealed.
 (b) The table of contents of the Natural Gas Policy Act of 1978 (15
 U.S.C. 3301 note) is amended by striking out the items relating to sections
 122 and 507.
TITLE V--FLEXIBILITY IN RESCHEDULING OR MARKING DOWN TROUBLED LOANS
SEC. 501. FLEXIBILITY IN RESCHEDULING OR MARKING DOWN TROUBLED LOANS.
 The Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, the Comptroller of
 the Currency, the Board of Directors of the Federal Deposit Insurance
 Corporation, the Federal Home Loan Bank Board, and the Federal Savings
 and Loan Insurance Corporation shall promulgate regulations granting
 depository institutions with energy-related loans greater flexibility in
 rescheduling or marking down troubled loans. The regulations shall also
 ease bank capital requirements to permit depository institutions greater
 flexibility in dealing with troubled loans.
