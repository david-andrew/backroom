 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United

SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE
 This Act may be cited as the `Clean Air Act Amendments of the 101st
 Congress'.
SEC. 2. TECHNOLOGY-BASED STANDARDS FOR HAZARDOUS AIR POLLUTANTS.
 Section 112 of the Clean Air Act is amended to read as follows:
`SEC. 112. HAZARDOUS AIR POLLUTANTS.
 `(a) DEFINITIONS- For the purpose of this section--
 `(1) The term `major source' means any stationary source (including all
 units of such source located within a contiguous area and under common
 control) that emits or has the potential to emit considering controls, in
 the aggregate, ten tons per year or more of any air pollutant or twenty-five
 tons per year or more of any combination of air pollutants which have been
 listed pursuant to subsection (b)(1). The Administrator may establish
 a lesser quantity, or in the case of radionuclides different criteria,
 for a major source other than that specified in the previous sentence,
 on the basis of the potency, characteristics of the air pollutant, or
 other relevant factors.
 `(2) The term `area source' means any member of a class of similar
 stationary sources of air pollutants that are not major sources but that,
 when considered together, emit, or have the potential to emit, on an
 area-wide basis, any air pollutant or pollutants which have been listed
 pursuant to subsection (b)(1).
 `(3) The term `new source' means a stationary source the construction
 of which is commenced after the Administrator proposes regulations under
 this section establishing an emission standard applicable to such source
 category or subcategory pursuant to subsection (d).
 `(4) The term, `stationary source', `modification', `owner or operator',
 and `existing' source shall have the same meaning as such terms have under
 section 111(a).

 section a list of air pollutants as follows:

 `(2)(A) The Administrator shall, from time to time, but not less often than
 every three years, review and revise the list established by paragraph (1).
 `(B)(i) Any person may petition the Administrator to modify the list by
 adding or deleting a substance. Within eighteen months after receipt of
 a petition the Administrator shall either grant or deny the petition by
 publishing a written explanation of the reasons for his decision. The
 Administrator may not deny a petition solely on the basis of inadequate
 resources or time for review. Any air pollutant which is included on the
 list under section 108(a), or which is regulated for a source category
 under section 111(d) may not be added to the list.
 `(ii) The Administrator shall add a substance to the list if the substance
 is an air pollutant that he finds, in his discretion, presents risks to
 public health warranting regulation under this section. He may delete a
 pollutant from the list if he finds, in his discretion, that it does not
 present such risks.
 `(iii) If the Administrator determines that information on the health effects
 of a substance is not sufficient to make a determination required by this
 subparagraph, the Administrator shall use the authorities of section 104(i)
 of the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation and Liability
 Act to acquire such information.
 `(c) SOURCE CATEGORY LIST- (1) Not later than twelve months after the
 date of enactment, the Administrator shall publish (and from time to time
 revise) a list of all categories and subcategories of major sources of the
 air pollutants listed pursuant to subsection (b). He shall simultaneously
 designate 50 per centum of the categories or subcategories for regulation
 under subsection (d), according to the schedule in subparagraps (e)(1)
 (A) and (B).
 `(2) For the remaining 50 per centum of the list, the Administrator shall,
 within five years after the date of enactment, review and designate those
 additional categories and subcategories that he finds, in his discretion,
 present risks warranting regulation under this section. For the categories,
 subcategories, or area sources he designates, he shall establish emission
 standards under subsection (d), according to the schedule in subparagraph
 (e)(1)(C).
 `(3) The Administrator may designate a category or subcategory of area
 sources that he finds, in his discretion, presents risks warranting
 regulation under this section for the establishment of emission standards
 under subsection (d), in accordance with paragraph (c)(2).
 `(4) The Administrator may designate any category or subcategory regulated,
 or considered for regulation, under this section as in effect prior to the
 enactment of this subsection, that he finds in his discretion, presents
 risks warranting regulation under this section, for the establishment of
 emission standards under subsection (d), in accordance with paragraph (c)(2).
 `(5) The Administrator may withdraw a designation under this subsection
 if he finds, in his discretion, that it does not present risks warranting
 regulation under this section.
 `(d) EMISSION STANDARDS- (1) The Administrator shall promulgate regulations
 establishing emission standards for each category or subcategory of major
 sources as specified under subsection (c). The Administrator may distinguish
 among classes, types and sizes of sources within a category or subcategory
 in establishing such standards.
 `(2) Emission standards promulgated under this subsection shall be applicable
 to new and existing major sources in each category or subcategory,
 and shall require the maximum degree of reduction in emissions of the
 air pollutants subject to this section (including a prohibition of such
 emissions, if achievable) that the administrator, taking into consideration
 the cost of achieving such emission reduction, and any non-air quality
 health and environmental impacts and energy requirements, determines is
 achievable through application of measures, processes, methods, systems,
 or techniques including, but not limited to, measures which--
 `(A) reduce the volume of such pollutants through process changes,
 substitution of materials or other modifications,
 `(B) enclose systems or processes to eliminate emissions, or
 `(C) collect, capture or treat such pollutants when released from a process,
 stack, storage or fugitive emissions point.
 `(D) are design, equipment, work practice or operational methods, or
 radiation dose standards, or
 `(E) are a combination of the above,
 `(3) The degree of reduction in emissions that is deemed achievable for
 new sources in a category or subcategory shall not be less stringent
 than the most stringent emissions level that is achieved in practice
 by a source in the same category or subcategory, as determined by the
 Administrator. Emission standards promulgated under this subsection for
 existing sources of a category or subcategory may be less stringent than
 standards for new sources in the same category or subcategory.
 `(4) Emission standards promulgated under this subsection shall be effective
 upon promulgation.
 `(5) After the effective date of any emission standard under this paragraph--
 `(A) No person may construct a new source or modify any existing source
 subject to emission standards under this paragraph unless the Administrator,
 or a State with an approved program under subsection (i), determines
 that such source, if properly constructed or operated, will comply with
 such standards.
 `(B) No person may operate an existing source subject to emission standards
 under this subsection unless the source complies with such standards not
 later than three years after the effective date.
 `(6) The Administrator shall from time to time review, and, if appropriate,
 revise emissions standards promulgated under this subsection.
 `(e) SCHEDULE FOR STANDARDS- (1) The Administrator shall promulgate
 standards for categories and subcategories designated under paragraph
 (c)(1) of this section according to the following schedule:
 `(A) Emission standards for not less than ten categories or subcategories
 shall be promulgated not later than two years after the date of enactment.
 `(B) Emission standards for 50 per centum of the designated categories
 or subcategories shall be promulgated not later than four years after the
 date of enactment;
 `(C) Emission standards for an additional 50 per centum of the designated
 categories or subcategories shall be promulgated not later than seven
 years after such date.
 `(2) Emission standards for the remaining categories and subcategories
 designated under paragraph (c)(2) of this section shall be promulgated
 not later than ten years after the date of enactment.
 `(3) In determining priorities for promulgating standards under subsection
 (d), the Administrator shall consider--
 `(A) the quantity and location of emissions of air pollutants subject to
 this section that each category or subcategory will emit;
 `(B) the known or anticipated adverse effects of such pollutants on public
 health; and
 `(C) the efficiency of grouping categories or subcategories according to
 the pollutants emitted, or the processes or technologies used.
 `(f) SIGNIFICANT RISK EVALUATION- (1) Within seven years after promulgation
 of emission standards for a category of sources pursuant to subsection (d),
 the Administrator shall evaluate the risks to the public health remaining
 after application of such standards.
 `(2) If the Administrator finds after such evaluation, that the category
 or subcategory, or portion thereof, after application of the prescribed
 standards, presents a significant risk of serious or widespread adverse
 effects on public health, he shall, within two years after completion of
 such evaluation, promulgate standards requiring an additional emissions
 reduction of 90 per centum unless he finds that a lesser reduction will
 reduce or eliminate such risk.
 `(3) Any standards promulgated under this subsection shall be effective
 upon the date of promulgation.
 `(4) After the effective date of any emission standard under this
 subsection--
 `(A) No person may construct a new source or modify an existing source
 subject to emission standards under this subsection unless the Administrator,
 or a State with a program approved under subsection (i), determines that such
 source, if properly constructed or operated, will comply with such standards.
 `(B) No person may operate an existing source subject to emission standards
 under this subsection unless the source complies with such standards not
 later than six years after the effective date.
 `(g) GRACE PERIOD- Notwithstanding the requirements of this section,
 no existing source that has installed controls required under section
 111, best available control technology (as defined in section 169(3)),
 reasonably available control technology, or technology required to meet a
 lowest achievable emission rate (as defined in Part D) shall be required to
 comply with emission standards applicable to such major source under this
 section until the date five years after the date on which such technology
 has been installed, as determined by the Administrator.
 `(h) VARIANCES AND EXTENSIONS- (1) Notwithstanding the requirements of
 subsections (d) and (f), the Administrator, or a State with a program
 approved under subsection (i), may issue a permit that grants a major
 source a variance from standards under this section, if the owner or
 operator presents evidence sufficient to demonstrate that emissions from
 the source present an insignificant risk to public health;
 `(2) The Administrator shall publish rules establishing minimum requirements
 for granting variances. The rules shall include an identification of
 categories or subcategories of sources listed under subsection (c) for
 which variances may not be granted, including categories or subcategories
 of area sources for which emission standards are promulgated to control
 combined emissions from sources, rather than emissions of individual sources.
 `(3) The Administrator shall also publish information and guidance on
 methods for assessing the significance of public health risks and methods
 for evaluating evidence presented by an owner or operator in support
 of proposed variances under this subsection. Any modeling demonstration
 performed to support a variance request shall comply with guidance issued by
 the Administrator. Any stack height credit used in a modeling demonstration
 shall be based on the lesser of the actual stack height of the source as
 it existed in January 1, 1988, or the GEP formula of H+1.5L.
 `(4) The Administrator, or a State with a program approved under subsection
 (1), may issue a permit that grants an extension permitting an existing
 source up to two additional years to comply with standards under subsection
 (d), if the administrator or the State, finds that such additional period
 is necessary for the installation of controls.
 `(i) PERMIT PROGRAMS- (1) After the date of enactment of this section,
 it shall be unlawful for any person to construct a new source or modify an
 existing source, or for the owner or operator of any major source to emit
 any air pollutant, subject to any emission standard under this section,
 except in compliance with a permit issued by a State or the Administrator
 under this section. The Administrator shall promulgate within 12 months
 after the date of enactment regulations establishing the minimum elements
 of a permit program. These elements shall include each of the following:
 `(A) Requirements for permit applications, including a standard application
 form.
 `(B) Monitoring and reporting requirements.
 `(C) A requirement that permittees pay a reasonable fee (annually
 or on a lump sum basis) sufficient to offset all reasonable costs of
 administering the program. If such fees are paid to the Administrator,
 he may, notwithstanding any requirement of the Miscellaneous Receipts
 Act, but subject to annual appropriations, expend them for the purpose of
 administering the program.
 `(D) Requirements for adequate personnel and funding to administer the
 program.
 `(E) A requirement that the State have adequate authority to:
 `(i) issue permits that apply, and assure compliance by all sources within
 the State with, each applicable standard, regulation or requirement under
 this section;
 `(ii) issue permits for a fixed term, not to exceed ten years;
 `(iii) terminate or modify permits for cause, including establishment of
 a new emission standard applicable to the source;
 `(iv) enforce permits and the requirement to obtain a permit, including
 adequate civil and criminal penalties; and
 `(v) ensure that no permit will be issued if the Administrator timely
 objects to its issuance.
 `(F) Adequate procedures for public notice, including an opportunity for
 public hearing, on any permit application.
 `(2) A single permit may be issued for a facility with multiple sources,
 including a permit that allows the operation of new or modified sources
 within such facility without further separate application procedures, if such
 permit assures compliance with the requirements of this section. When issuing
 permits in the absence of an approved State program, the Administrator
 shall comply with the guidelines issued under paragraph (1).
 `(3) Each State may develop and submit to the Administrator for approval a
 permit program under this section. Not later than one hundred and eighty
 days after receiving a program submitted by a State, and after notice
 and opportunity for public comment, the Administrator shall approve such
 program, in whole or in part, unless he finds that the program fails to
 meet the requirements of this section, including the regulations issued
 under paragraph (1). If the Administrator disapproves a State program,
 he shall notify the State of any revisions or modifications necessary to
 obtain approval. The State may revise and resubmit the proposed program
 for review under this section.
 `(4) The Administrator shall suspend the issuance of permits by the Agency
 promptly upon approval of a State program under this section.
 `(5)(A) Each State shall transmit to the Administrator a copy of each
 permit application, including any application for a variance, submitted
 under this section, and shall provide notice of every action related to
 the consideration of the application, including each permit proposed to
 be issued by the State.
 `(B) No permit shall be issued if the Administrator within sixty days objects
 in writing to its issuance as not in compliance with the requirements of
 this section. The Administrator shall provide with the objection a statement
 of the reasons for the objection and the terms and conditions that the
 Administrator would impose if the permit were issued by the Administrator.
 `(C) If the State fails within ninety days after the date of the objection
 to submit a permit revised to meet the objection, the Administrator shall
 have authority to issue or deny the permit.
 `(6) Whenever the Administrator determines that a State is not administering
 and enforcing a program, or portion thereof, approved pursuant to this
 subsection in accordance with the requirements of this section, he shall
 so notify the State and, if action which will assure prompt compliance is
 not taken within ninety days, the Administrator shall withdraw approval
 of the program or portion. The Administrator shall not withdraw approval
 of any program unless the State shall have been notified in writing of
 the reasons for withdrawal.
 `(7) Nothing in this subsection shall prohibit the Administrator from
 enforcing any applicable emission standard or requirement under this
 section, provided that, until December 31, 1992, if a permit required
 by this subsection has been applied for, but final action has not been
 taken on the application, the source's failure to have a permit shall
 not be a violation of this subsection, unless the Administrator or other
 plaintiff proves that the delay in final action was due to the failure of
 the applicant timely to submit information reasonably required or requested
 to process the application.
 `(8) The Administrator shall establish and maintain an air toxics
 clearinghouse, control technology center, and risk information center to
 provide technical assistance and information to the States on measures,
 methods, practices and techniques effective in reducing the emissions of
 air pollutants subject to this section.
 `(9) Upon application of a State, the Administrator may make grants, subject
 to such terms and conditions as the Administrator deems appropriate, to such
 State for the purpose of assisting the State in developing and implementing a
 program for submittal and approval under this subsection. Programs assisted
 under this paragraph may include elements addressing air pollutants other
 than those specifically subject to this section.
 `(j) EFFECT ON OTHER PROVISIONS- Nothing in this section shall be construed
 to exempt a source subject to standards under this section from standards
 or requirements established under any other section of this Act.
 `(k) PRESIDENTIAL EXEMPTION- The President may exempt any source from
 compliance with standards or requirements under this section for a period of
 not more than two years if the President finds that the technology or means
 to comply with such standards or requirements are not available, and the
 operation of such source is required for reasons of national security. An
 exemption under this paragraph may be extended for one or more additional
 periods, each period not to exceed two years. The President shall make a
 report to Congress with respect to each exemption (or extension thereof)
 made under this paragraph.
 `(l) SAVINGS PROVISION- Any standards promulgated under this section as
 in effect prior to the enactment of this subsection shall remain in force,
 as promulgated, unless modified in accordance with this section.
 `(m) APPROPRIATIONS- There are authorized to be appropriated such amounts
 as are necessary to carry out the requirements of this section.'.
